Research

Reading a black hole's heartbeat in scattered light.

My research interest is the multiwavelength study of Active Galactic Nuclei — their variability and the accretion disk physics behind it. Below is a walk-through of the three projects that have shaped that interest, from archival UV spectroscopy to my current Master's thesis.

01 — Current

Type-I Quasar Accretion Disk Variability

National Centre for Radio Astrophysics (NCRA-TIFR)
Master's Thesis Intern · May 2026 – Present

My ongoing thesis studies variability in Type-I quasar accretion disks using time-series photometry from the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) combined with survey data from SDSS. The goal is to connect observed brightness fluctuations to physical properties of the accretion disk.

02 — 2025

STARSFLUX: an all-sky catalogue of absolute spectro-photometric calibrators from 0.3 to 30 μm

Leiden University, Netherlands
Remote Research Intern · May 2025 – Dec 2025

Validated a structured catalog of absolute spectral-flux calibrators for VLTI/MATISSE and future infrared instruments, enabling more precise calibration for high-resolution interferometric observations. This involved a comparative analysis of the established Cohen et al. calibrators against newer flux models, using Python and Pandas, with Matplotlib visualizations built for spectral comparison, anomaly detection, and model validation.

03 — 2024

Study of UV Line and Continuum Variabilities in the Broadline Seyfert 1 Galaxy ESO 141–G55

Indian Institute of Technology, Hyderabad
Remote Research Intern · Jun 2024 – Feb 2025

I modelled and analysed 53 archival ultraviolet spectra of the Seyfert galaxy ESO 141–G55, drawn from the International Ultraviolet Explorer (IUE) satellite. Using JAVELIN to perform reverberation mapping, I traced the galaxy's variability, and used HEASOFT for data reduction and modelling — fitting power-law and Lorentzian models to spectral features, and quantifying continuum and emission-line flux with the cflux model. This work became my first publication, in Astronomische Nachrichten (2026).
